Clinical Reflections

Developing Assessment and Therapeutic Skills in Child and Adolescent Mental Health

Haumaru Orite • Child and Family Unit, Starship, Auckland City Hospital


During my placement in the Child and Family Unit, I supported adolescents experiencing acute mental distress. This experience highlighted the importance of creating safe spaces where young people feel respected, listened to, and supported.


Through therapeutic communication, I developed my ability to build trusting relationships with tamariki and whānau. I learned that effective mental health nursing involves more than completing assessments. It requires patience, empathy, and understanding each person's unique experiences.


I progressed from observing Mental State Examinations to completing comprehensive assessments and structured risk assessments under supervision. This strengthened my confidence in clinical reasoning and reinforced the importance of recovery focused and least restrictive care.


This placement contributed to my growth in Whanaungatanga, Manaakitanga, and Pūkengatanga by developing my communication skills, clinical judgement, and ability to provide safe and supportive care.

Recognising Deterioration and Advocating for Patient Safety in Paediatric Medical Care

Kidz First Medical Ward • Middlemore Hospital


My placement at Kidz First strengthened my ability to assess and respond to the changing needs of tamariki with a range of acute and chronic conditions.


A key learning experience was developing confidence in recognising early signs of deterioration through systematic assessment and use of the Early Warning Score. I learned the importance of acting promptly, communicating concerns clearly, and seeking support when required.


Working alongside Registered Nurses and the wider multidisciplinary team strengthened my understanding of advocacy and accountability. I recognised that safe nursing care involves not only clinical knowledge but also ensuring children and their whānau feel included and supported throughout their healthcare journey.


This experience developed my skills in clinical reasoning, prioritisation, communication, and patient advocacy while reinforcing the importance of family centred care.

Supporting Recovery Through Assessment and Education in Paediatric Surgical Care

Orthopaedics Ward 24A • Starship, Auckland City Hospital


During my surgical placement, I developed my understanding of post operative nursing care for tamariki following orthopaedic procedures. I focused on assessment, pain management, wound care, and supporting recovery.


Completing wound assessments, monitoring recovery progress, and assessing pain using validated tools strengthened my attention to detail and ability to identify changes requiring further review.


I also supported tamariki and whānau through education about wound care, mobility, and recovery expectations. This showed me the importance of clear communication and ensuring families feel confident and involved in the care process.


This placement strengthened my development in Manaakitanga and Pūkengatanga by combining clinical skills with compassionate, family centred communication.

Cultural Safety & Te Tiriti o Waitangi

As a nurse in Aotearoa New Zealand, I recognise Te Tiriti o Waitangi as fundamental to providing safe, equitable, and culturally responsive healthcare. Kawa Whakaruruhau guides my commitment to understanding the impact of culture, power, and personal beliefs on nursing practice.


I understand that culturally safe care begins with self reflection and recognising each person as an individual with their own values, experiences, beliefs, and identity. Throughout my clinical placements, I have aimed to provide care that respects patients and whānau while supporting their involvement in healthcare decisions.

Partnership

Working alongside Māori patients and whānau through respectful communication, shared decision making, and recognising their knowledge of their own health experiences.

Active Protection

Supporting equitable health outcomes by providing culturally safe care, reflecting on personal biases, and advocating against barriers to healthcare.

Participation

Encouraging Māori patients and whānau to actively participate in planning and decisions about their healthcare.

Options

Respecting individual preferences and supporting access to healthcare pathways that align with cultural values and needs.

Tino Rangatiratanga

Respecting Māori self determination and supporting patients to have control and involvement in decisions affecting their health and wellbeing.

These principles guide my nursing practice across mental health, medical, surgical, and community settings. I recognise that culturally safe practice requires ongoing learning, humility, and reflection. As a graduate nurse, I will continue developing my understanding of Te Tiriti o Waitangi and apply these principles to provide respectful and equitable care.
